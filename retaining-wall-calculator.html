How the estimate works

The calculator divides the face area of the wall by the face area of one block, then adds your waste allowance. It also shows how many courses (rows) and how many blocks per course, each rounded up.

Wall face area = length × height Block face area = block length × block height ÷ 144 (square feet, with block sizes in inches) Blocks to buy = round up (wall area ÷ block area × (1 + waste ÷ 100)) Courses = round up (wall height in inches ÷ block height)

Worked example

A wall 20 ft long and 3 ft high, built from blocks 16 inches long and 8 inches high, with 10% waste.

  1. Wall face area = 20 × 3 = 60 sq ft.
  2. Block face area = 16 × 8 ÷ 144 = 0.889 sq ft.
  3. Blocks before waste = 60 ÷ 0.889 = 67.5.
  4. With 10% waste = 67.5 × 1.10 = 74.25, rounded up to 75 blocks.
  5. Courses = round up (36 ÷ 8) = 5. Blocks per course = round up (240 ÷ 16) = 15.

What this estimate is, and is not

  • It is a basic material estimate. It is not an engineering or structural safety assessment.
  • It assumes a straight wall with no corners, curves, steps or stepped-back courses.
  • It does not include cap blocks, base gravel, drainage pipe, backfill, geogrid or other reinforcement.
  • Walls can fail if they are not designed for the soil, water and loads behind them. Ask your local building department and, when needed, a qualified engineer.

Before you buy

  • Measure along the ground on a slope, not in a straight line through the air.
  • Order a few extra blocks for cuts at the ends and corners.
  • Ask your block supplier for the installation guide. It covers base depth, drainage and when you need an engineer.

Frequently asked questions

Where do I find the block size?

Look at the product label or the manufacturer’s data sheet. Use the face dimensions of the block as it sits in the wall. Nominal sizes can differ slightly from actual sizes.

Should I include a buried first course?

Many block makers tell you to bury the first course below grade. If yours does, include the buried height in the wall height so the block count covers it. Follow your block maker’s instructions.

Do I need a permit or an engineer?

Rules vary by place and wall height. Many areas require a permit or an engineered design above a certain height, often around 4 feet, and walls that hold back a slope, support a driveway or building, or sit near a property line can need approval at any height. Ask your local building department before you build.

Does this include cap blocks, gravel and drainage?

No. Cap blocks, base material, drainage pipe and backfill are separate items. Use the gravel calculator on this site to estimate base or backfill volume.

Why are there two different block counts?

The main result divides area by block size, which is the best estimate of the total. The courses and blocks-per-course figures are each rounded up, which shows the grid you will build and why you will need some cut blocks.
