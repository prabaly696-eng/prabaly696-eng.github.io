How the post count works

Posts hold the fence up at each end of every section. A straight run with N sections needs N + 1 posts, because both ends of the fence need a post. A closed fence that returns to its starting point needs only N posts, because the last section meets the first post.

Sections = round up (fence length ÷ maximum post spacing) Open run: posts = sections + 1 Closed loop: posts = sections Posts to buy = round up (posts × (1 + spare allowance ÷ 100)) Even spacing = fence length ÷ sections

The spacing you enter is treated as the maximum distance between posts. Rounding the number of sections up keeps every section at or under that distance.

Worked example

A straight fence 100 ft long with posts no more than 8 ft apart.

  1. Sections = round up (100 ÷ 8) = round up 12.5 = 13.
  2. Open run: posts = 13 + 1 = 14 posts.
  3. Even spacing = 100 ÷ 13 = 7.69 ft, which is under the 8 ft limit.
  4. With a 5% spare allowance: 14 × 1.05 = 14.7, rounded up to 15 posts.

If the same 100 ft of fence formed a closed loop, it would need 13 sections and 13 posts.

What is not counted

  • Rails, pickets, panels, gates, gate hardware, brackets, concrete and fasteners. This calculator needs more details to estimate those, so it does not guess.
  • Extra posts for corners, gates, changes of direction, steep slopes or wind loads. Count these separately.
  • Local rules. Fence height, setbacks from property lines, materials and permits vary by city, county and neighborhood association.

Before you buy

  • Mark the post positions with stakes and string before you buy. Spacing often changes at gates and corners.
  • Check property lines and any neighborhood rules before you dig.
  • Concrete or gravel for the post holes is a separate item. Ask your supplier how much each hole needs.

Frequently asked questions

Why is there one more post than sections?

Every section needs a post at both ends. In a straight run, the posts between sections are shared, but the two end posts are not, so the total is sections plus one.

What post spacing should I use?

It depends on the fence style and the length of the panels or rails. Many wood fences use posts about 6 to 8 feet apart. Use the spacing your panels, rails or manufacturer call for.

Does this count corner and gate posts?

No. Corners, gates and changes of direction often need extra or heavier posts. Count them separately and follow the instructions for your fence.

What if my fence has several straight sections?

Run the calculator once for each straight section as an open run, and add the results. Posts shared at corners would then be counted twice, so subtract one for each shared corner.

Do I need to check for utilities before digging?

Yes. In the United States, call 811 a few days before you dig so underground utility lines can be marked for free.
