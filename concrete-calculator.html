How the volume is calculated

A flat slab is a box, so its volume is length × width × thickness. The calculator changes every measurement to feet, finds the volume in cubic feet, then divides by 27 to get cubic yards (or by 35.31 for cubic meters). It then adds your extra allowance for spills and uneven ground.

Volume (cu ft) = length (ft) × width (ft) × thickness (ft) × number of slabs where thickness in feet = inches ÷ 12 Volume (cu yd) = cubic feet ÷ 27 Volume to order = volume × (1 + extra ÷ 100) Bags = round up (volume to order ÷ yield of one bag)

Worked example

A 10 ft by 10 ft slab, 4 inches thick, with 10% extra. The bag yield of 0.6 cubic feet is an example only. Read the real number on the bag you plan to use.

  1. Area = 10 × 10 = 100 sq ft.
  2. Thickness = 4 ÷ 12 = 0.333 ft.
  3. Volume = 100 × 0.333 = 33.3 cubic ft = 1.235 cubic yards.
  4. With 10% extra = 33.3 × 1.10 = 36.7 cubic ft = 1.36 cubic yards.
  5. Bags = round up (36.7 ÷ 0.6) = round up 61.1 = 62 bags.

Assumptions and limits

  • The slab is a flat rectangle with the same thickness everywhere. For several identical slabs, enter the number of slabs.
  • This is the volume of concrete only. It does not include the gravel base, rebar or mesh, forms, sealer or finishing tools.
  • The bag yield changes with the product and with how much water is added. Always use the number printed on the bag.
  • It does not decide how thick or how strong a slab should be. Driveways, garage floors and anything that carries heavy loads need a proper design. Ask your local building department.

Before you buy

  • Measure the thickness at the edges and in the middle. A dip in the ground takes more concrete.
  • Order a little extra. Running short in the middle of a pour is hard to fix.
  • Weather matters. Very hot, cold or wet days change how concrete sets.

Frequently asked questions

How thick should a concrete slab be?

It depends on the use. A walkway or patio slab is often about 4 inches (10 cm) thick, while driveways and floors that carry heavy loads are usually thicker and may need reinforcement. Follow the plan for your project and your local code.

How much extra concrete should I order?

Many people add about 5% to 10% for spills, uneven ground and over-digging. Running short in the middle of a pour is harder to fix than having a little left over.

How many bags of concrete are in a cubic yard?

An 80 pound bag of mix makes roughly 0.6 cubic feet, so one cubic yard (27 cubic feet) needs about 45 bags. Different bag sizes and brands vary, so check the yield printed on the bag.

Should I use bags or ready-mix delivery?

Bags are practical for small jobs. For larger pours, ready-mix delivered by truck is usually ordered by volume. Ask the supplier about minimum loads, delivery fees and how long you have to place the concrete.

Does this include the gravel base and rebar?

No. This is the volume of concrete only. Use the gravel calculator on this site to estimate the base, and ask your supplier about reinforcement.
