How the estimate works

Deck boards are laid in rows. The calculator first finds how many rows fit across the deck, then how many boards each row needs, then adds your waste allowance. Every result is rounded up to whole boards.

Rows = round up ((distance across the boards in inches + gap) ÷ (board width + gap)) Boards per row = round up (row length ÷ board length) Boards to buy = round up (rows × boards per row × (1 + waste ÷ 100))

Worked example

A 16 ft by 12 ft deck. The boards are 5.5 inches wide with a 0.25 inch gap, they are 12 ft long and they run along the 16 ft side. Waste allowance is 10%.

  1. The rows must cover the 12 ft side, which is 144 inches.
  2. Rows = round up (144 + 0.25) ÷ (5.5 + 0.25) = round up 25.09 = 26 rows.
  3. Each row is 16 ft long. Boards per row = round up (16 ÷ 12) = 2 boards.
  4. Boards before waste = 26 × 2 = 52.
  5. With 10% waste = 52 × 1.10 = 57.2, rounded up to 58 boards.

With 16 ft boards, each row needs only 1 board, so the total is 26 rows × 1 = 26 boards, or 29 boards with waste. Board length changes the count a lot, so compare the lengths your supplier sells.

Assumptions and limits

  • The deck is a simple rectangle, and every board runs in a straight line in the direction you choose.
  • Each row is cut from whole boards. Offcuts are not reused, except through the waste allowance you enter.
  • Board width means the actual width, not the nominal size. A 5/4×6 or 2×6 decking board is about 5.5 inches wide.
  • This counts surface boards only. It does not include joists, beams, posts, footings, fasteners, railings, stairs or fascia.
  • It does not check whether the deck is safe or meets building code. Ask your local building department about permits and structural requirements.
  • Diagonal layouts, picture-frame borders and curved decks use more material than this calculator assumes.

Before you buy

  • Buy all boards in one order if you can. Color can differ between production batches.
  • Ask your supplier which board lengths they stock. Longer boards can mean fewer joints.
  • Maximum joist spacing depends on the decking product. Check the installation guide.

Frequently asked questions

What width should I enter for a decking board?

Enter the actual width, not the nominal size. A 5/4×6 or 2×6 board is about 5.5 inches wide, and a 2×4 is about 3.5 inches wide. Check the label or the maker’s data for your product.

What gap should I leave between deck boards?

Follow the decking manufacturer’s instructions. Gaps are often around 1/8 to 1/4 inch, but wood, composite and PVC products can differ.

How much waste should I add?

Many installers add about 10% for a straight layout and more for diagonal or picture-frame patterns. Your supplier can tell you what is typical for your product.

Does this include joists and fasteners?

No. This calculator counts surface boards only. Joists, beams, posts, footings, screws, hidden fasteners and railings need their own plan.

Why does the board length setting change the answer?

Each row is cut from whole boards, so a 16 foot board covers a 16 foot row in one piece while a 12 foot board needs a second board. Choosing the length that wastes the least is one way to save money.
